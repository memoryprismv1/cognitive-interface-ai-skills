# Automaticity

**Skill:** `automaticity`

**Purpose:** Write responses so that the reader can spend as little
conscious effort as possible decoding the language, leaving more
cognitive capacity available for understanding, reasoning, comparing,
and thinking.

**Core rule:**

> **Simplify the form. Never simplify the idea.**

This skill is based on a biological property of skilled reading: parts
of language processing become highly practiced and automatic. When
wording, vocabulary, or sentence structure is unfamiliar or demanding,
additional cognitive resources are required. The purpose of this skill
is therefore not to make ideas simpler. It is to keep the *linguistic
interface* inside the reader's practiced, relatively automatic range
whenever possible.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. The biological basis

Reading is a learned skill.

With practice, the brain becomes increasingly efficient at translating
written symbols into language. This is not merely a metaphor:
neurophysiological work shows that skilled readers can recruit language
systems automatically in response to written words.

A magnetoencephalography study of children learning to read found
word-selective responses in superior temporal cortex beginning at about
300 ms after word presentation, even when attention was directed toward
a competing fixation task. The strength of this automatic response was
related to reading skill. The authors describe automatic recruitment of
spoken-language circuits as a hallmark of skilled reading and argue that
practice makes the translation from visual symbols toward speech and
meaning increasingly effortless.

**Key point:** some components of reading can become sufficiently
practiced that they require little conscious attention.

Reference:

-   Turker et al. (2021), *Automaticity in the reading circuitry*.
    Neuropsychologia. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33516066/

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Automatic does not mean that all reading is automatic

Reading is not one indivisible operation.

Research separates processes including lexical access, syntactic
processing, semantic integration, working memory, and higher-level
comprehension. These processes can have different demands.

An fMRI study independently manipulated **lexical frequency** and
**syntactic complexity** during sentence comprehension. Both factors
changed activity in the left-hemisphere language network, and high
lexical and syntactic demands produced larger effects across a
distributed network. The study also found differences between people
with higher and lower language working-memory capacity in neural
efficiency, adaptability, and synchronization.

This matters because a reader can have highly practiced word recognition
while still encountering effort at a later linguistic stage.

Reference:

-   Keller, Carpenter & Just (2001), *The neural bases of sentence
    comprehension: a fMRI examination of syntactic and lexical
    processing*. Cerebral Cortex.
    https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11230094/

Related analysis:

-   Prat, Keller & Just (2007), *Individual Differences in Sentence
    Comprehension: A Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging Investigation
    of Syntactic and Lexical Processing Demands*.
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2599910/

**Key point:** there is no single global switch between "easy language"
and "hard language." Different parts of language processing can impose
different loads.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Reading load can be measured with EEG

The distinction is not only theoretical.

An EEG study of reading found that different components of the neural
response changed in opposite directions when the task was repeated.
N1-P2 responses indicated reduced resource demands for lower-level
processes such as word recognition and local comprehension, while P300
and reaction-time measures reflected increased demands from higher-level
processes such as comparing the text with a prior representation and
updating memory.

The authors explicitly interpreted the result as support for a
**componential description of reading and task difficulty**.

Reference:

-   *Monitoring changes in cognitive load during reading: an
    event-related brain potential and reaction time analysis* (1993).
    https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8423433/

**Key point:** reading load is not simply "more difficult text = more
brain activity." Different processing layers can become more or less
demanding independently.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Easy and difficult prose have measurable EEG differences

This has also been tested with actual texts rather than isolated words.

An EEG study recorded normal and dyslexic readers while they read **easy
and difficult texts**, both silently and aloud. This directly
demonstrates that prose difficulty can be studied through EEG rather
than inferred only from subjective reports.

Reference:

-   Galin et al. (1992), *EEG spectra in dyslexic and normal readers
    during oral and silent reading*.
    https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1370788/

Other EEG work has combined neural measures with behavioral measures of
reading difficulty and cognitive workload.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Cognitive load increases when linguistic processing becomes demanding

Sentence structure matters independently of vocabulary.

Research using fMRI has shown that syntactic complexity changes
activation in the language network. Research using behavioral and
neuroimaging methods also shows that working-memory capacity affects how
people handle increasingly complex linguistic structures.

The practical implication is not that long sentences are inherently bad.
It is that **unnecessary linguistic complexity can move processing away
from highly practiced routines and increase the resources required to
construct the meaning.**

This is why the skill does not use a fixed sentence-length rule as its
scientific basis.

A short sentence can be cognitively awkward.

A longer sentence can be completely automatic for a practiced reader.

The target is **processing friction**, not word count.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Alzheimer's research provides an important dissociation

Research on Alzheimer's disease is useful here because deterioration
does not simply erase "reading" as one ability.

A classic study found that reading aloud remained preserved in all but
the most severely impaired patients, while reading comprehension
declined progressively with dementia severity. The authors concluded
that the comprehension disturbance was linguistic rather than simply
visual-perceptual.

Reference:

-   Cummings, Houlihan & Hill (1986), *The pattern of reading
    deterioration in dementia of the Alzheimer type: observations and
    implications*. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3790984/

Other studies have demonstrated partially separable reading routes.
Patients with probable Alzheimer's disease could, in some cases, read
words aloud accurately despite severe semantic impairment, supporting
models in which lexical/orthographic-to-phonological processing can
operate without full semantic mediation.

Reference:

-   *Reading lexically without semantics: evidence from patients with
    probable Alzheimer's disease* (1997).
    https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9375183/

This does **not** prove a specific "fallback language mode." It does
support the broader biological premise that reading contains separable
components and that those components can be differentially preserved or
impaired.

## 7. What this means for AI communication

The biological evidence supports the following chain:

``` text
Reading is a learned skill
        ↓
Practice makes some linguistic operations highly automatic
        ↓
Not every linguistic operation is equally automatic
        ↓
Lexical, syntactic, semantic, working-memory and higher-level
processes can impose different demands
        ↓
Unnecessary linguistic complexity can consume processing resources
        ↓
Reducing unnecessary linguistic complexity can leave more capacity
available for the underlying task
```

The final step is an **engineering inference**, not a claim that
neuroscience has already tested this exact AI response skill.

No study cited here demonstrates that "this exact AI response skill"
itself improves cognition.

What the research demonstrates is the biological substrate on which the
skill is based: **language processing has automatic and effortful
components, and the demand placed on those components is measurable.**

The AI-use skill is an application of that finding.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. What the skill does

The skill tries to keep the *form* of the response inside the reader's
practiced linguistic routines.

### Prefer

-   common words when they express the idea accurately;
-   familiar sentence structures;
-   short, direct sentences where that reduces processing friction;
-   one main idea per sentence when possible;
-   concrete examples before abstract explanations when the example
    makes the mechanism easier to grasp;
-   a technical term when it is genuinely useful, followed by its
    meaning in ordinary language;
-   plain verbs;
-   stable and predictable structure;
-   direct answers before supporting explanation.

### Avoid

-   unnecessary specialist terminology;
-   academic framing when it adds no information;
-   stacked abstract nouns;
-   long chains of nested clauses;
-   unnecessary hedging;
-   terminology used merely to signal expertise;
-   rewriting a simple idea into specialist language;
-   removing substantive content merely to make prose shorter.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. What this skill does NOT mean

### It does not mean "write for children."

The target is not a school grade.

There is no established universal human reading grade at which language
becomes automatic.

Automaticity depends on experience, familiarity, language knowledge,
reading skill, and the particular linguistic operation being performed.

### It does not mean "make everything simple."

A response can contain extremely advanced concepts.

The objective is:

> **advanced idea + low unnecessary linguistic friction**

not:

> **simple idea + simple words**

### It does not mean "shorter is always better."

Removing information can make a response easier to read while making it
harder to understand.

The rule is:

> **Cut processing friction, not substance.**

## 10. The motor-skill analogy

Language is a learned skill, so a useful analogy is motor learning.

A practiced motor action can become automatic while more complex or
unfamiliar movements still require conscious control. Learning a new
motor routine does not necessarily erase older routines; different
routines can remain available and be selected by context.

Reading has an analogous property: practiced linguistic operations can
become automatic while less practiced operations continue to require
attention.

This analogy is **a model for thinking about the skill, not evidence
that language literally uses the same neural architecture as motor
control**.

The research evidence establishes automaticity and separable processing
demands in reading. The motor analogy helps explain why the skill should
target the boundary between automatic and effortful processing.

## 11. Why this belongs in Cognitive Interface AI Skills

This is not a personality preference.

"Make it sound friendly," "don't be annoying," and "use my preferred
tone" are not sufficient grounds for inclusion here.

A skill belongs in this collection when:

1.  it targets a property of human cognition or biology;
2.  that property has empirical support;
3.  the AI behavior is a defensible application of that property;
4.  the README clearly separates **what research demonstrates** from
    **what the AI-use skill infers**.

For `automaticity`, the empirical basis is the existence of
automatic and effortful components of reading and the measurable effect
of lexical, syntactic, memory, and task demands on those components.

The AI application is to reduce unnecessary linguistic demand while
preserving conceptual content.

## 12. Evidence boundary

This skill makes a deliberately modest claim.

**Supported:**

-   skilled reading contains highly practiced, automatic components;
-   word recognition and language-network recruitment can become
    automatic;
-   lexical frequency and syntactic complexity alter neural processing
    demands;
-   working-memory capacity changes how the language network responds to
    those demands;
-   EEG can detect changes associated with different reading/task
    demands;
-   reading abilities contain partially separable components;
-   neurological disease can impair some reading components while
    leaving others relatively preserved.

**Not established by these studies:**

-   a universal "automatic reading grade level";
-   a universal vocabulary threshold for low cognitive load;
-   that every short sentence has lower cognitive load than every long
    sentence;
-   that this exact AI skill has been clinically or experimentally
    validated;
-   that reduced neural activation is always better;
-   that one writing style is automatically optimal for every reader.

The skill is therefore a **biologically grounded engineering rule**, not
a medical or neuroscientific prescription.

## 13. Selective visual promotion: bold and italics as interface cues

Making Vision uses **promotion** for a specific architectural function: a candidate that was previously treated cheaply or as background is allocated additional processing. The transition can be represented as:

```text
background → candidate → promoted candidate/event
```

Promotion does not require complete identification. A candidate can warrant investigation before its identity or meaning is settled. Promotion is also not identical to generic attention, and not every perturbation or novel item is promoted. A familiar object can become relevant because its state, movement, behaviour, or relation changes.

This skill applies that idea to written information. In a text stream, bold and italics can act as **visual interface cues** that make selected parts easier to locate during scanning. They are implementation choices, not biological mechanisms and not scientifically established equivalents of visual object promotion.

### A provisional convention

- **Bold** can mark the primary object, state, event, action, decision, or takeaway that matters in the current context.
- *Italics* can mark a secondary property, condition, contrast, relation, or qualifier that helps explain the promoted item.
- The target need not be the object's name. It may be a changed state, unexpected behaviour, or consequential relationship.
- Use emphasis selectively. If every noun, technical term, or sentence is marked, the distinction disappears.
- Formatting must not substitute for explicit explanation or overstate the certainty, urgency, or importance of a claim.
- Do not treat ALL-CAPS as a neutral equivalent. In online communication, capitalization can carry learned social meanings such as shouting, anger, insistence, or rudeness. Its function must be interpreted in context.
- Bold and italics also carry learned conventions and can be ambiguous. Their usefulness is reader- and context-dependent.

For example:

> The traffic light is familiar, but its **state has changed to red**; approaching vehicles must *stop*.

The emphasis promotes the relevant state and action rather than merely marking the familiar object name. Another context might call for a different target.

### Evidence boundary

Making Vision supplies the architectural inspiration: background → candidate → promotion; promotion can precede identification; and a familiar object can become relevant through a change in state or relation. The research does **not** establish that bold and italics reproduce this biological process or that a particular formatting scheme improves reading. Those are hypotheses for interface design.

A useful test would compare equivalent passages with no emphasis, selective bold, and selective bold-plus-italics, then measure whether readers find the relevant object/state/relation more accurately or quickly without increasing confusion or perceived emotional tone.

## 9. Research on AI-generated language and processing friction

Clark, T. H., Martin, M. B., & Mahowald, K. (2026). *Why Is AI-Generated Language So Hard to Understand?* https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29162

This preprint compares language from nine AI models with human writing and examines features associated with processing difficulty, including noun pileups, omitted complementisers such as “that,” long-distance dependencies, and unusual combinations of inanimate subjects with action verbs. The authors report that these patterns occur more often in model outputs than in human writing, with variation across models.

The paper also reports a controlled proof-of-concept experiment using Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. During reinforcement-learning fine-tuning on a structured-to-English task, a penalty for longer responses encouraged shorter constructions, including more frequent omission of optional “that.” The authors interpret this as evidence that optimisation for compression can influence grammatical choices even when the task does not require those choices.

### Relevance to Automaticity

The paper offers a plausible mechanism for the problem this skill addresses: a model can be rewarded for task success or token economy without being rewarded for making meaning easy for a person to recover. **Concision and communicative efficiency are not the same objective.**

Automaticity's rule remains:

> **Simplify the form. Never simplify the idea.**

The aim is to reduce avoidable processing friction while preserving the relationships and information needed to understand the idea. It does not mean making every sentence short or removing necessary technical content.

### Evidence boundary

The linguistic measurements are indicators associated with processing difficulty; they do not prove that every sentence containing these features is hard to understand. The controlled experiment uses a small model and a specific task. It does not establish that length penalties are the sole cause of frontier models' writing styles, nor does it test this Automaticity skill.

This is a research-informed design rationale, not a validation result. A direct test would compare meaning-matched ordinary model answers and Automaticity-style answers, measuring comprehension, reading time, recall, and preservation of substantive information.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Status

**Evidence status:** Biologically grounded; AI application informed by established reading/cognitive-load research and recent research on AI-generated language.

**Claim strength:** The underlying reading and linguistic phenomena have empirical support. The specific AI-writing application remains an engineering hypothesis to be tested.

**Core principle:**

> **Make the language automatic enough that the reader can spend their attention on the idea.**
