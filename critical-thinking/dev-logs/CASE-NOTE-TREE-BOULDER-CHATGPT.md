# Case Note: The Tree, the Boulder, and the Missing Causal Timeline

**Assistant:** ChatGPT  
**Inquiry type:** Image interpretation; causal reconstruction; provenance verification  
**Outcome:** The source post explicitly labelled the clip “Made with AI.” The depicted tree-and-boulder arrangement was not established as a real botanical specimen.

## Initial question

The user supplied a screenshot of a small, dramatically curved tree apparently supporting or growing around a large boulder and asked how this could happen. The original question was exploratory: find plausible explanations for the observed arrangement.

## What the assistant did wrong

The assistant initially privileged a natural-growth explanation and offered possible mechanisms—growth around an existing boulder, a rock falling onto a sapling, and perspective making the load look greater than it was—without first checking whether a coherent timeline could produce the particular configuration.

When the user challenged the growth trajectory, the assistant continued generating possibilities instead of reconstructing the developmental sequence. It did not initially account for:

- the stem's juvenile or slender appearance versus the implied history of becoming load-bearing;
- when the curvature would have been established and how the trunk would strengthen;
- why a growing shoot would follow a constrained route under or beside a boulder while still developing a viable, light-accessing crown;
- whether the boulder was actually supported by the tree, by the lower rocks, or by some hidden arrangement;
- the possibility that the scene was deliberately constructed, staged, or AI-generated.

The assistant then searched for loosely related bonsai techniques and contemporary tree art. These established, at most, that trunks can be trained into unusual shapes. They did not establish that the depicted tree was real, that the tree supported the boulder, or that a particular load-conditioning process had been used. The assistant treated thematic resemblance as corroboration.

## User's diagnostic contribution

The user identified a “ledger mismatch”: an explanation of the final configuration needed a complete causal timeline. The central objections were about the intermediate states, not just the final appearance:

1. **Mechanical/developmental ledger:** how could a juvenile stem acquire the necessary geometry and strength, and at what point would it carry the boulder's load?
2. **Growth/viability ledger:** why would the growing tip follow a path beneath the boulder, where the developing crown could be obstructed or deprived of light, instead of growing toward open space?
3. **Construction-history ledger:** if the boulder was placed only after the trunk was trained and strengthened, the final image concealed a deliberate assembly sequence that the natural-growth explanation had not accounted for.

These questions did not by themselves prove fabrication. They made the natural-growth story insufficient and should have triggered a comparison of natural growth, human construction, staging, and synthetic media.

## Provenance resolution

The user located the post by Science girl (@sciencegirl), captioned “Survival, despite the burdens of life,” with video credit to Feiyan Xie. The supplied screenshot visibly displayed the platform label **“Made with AI.”** This resolved the provenance question for the circulated clip: it was presented as AI-generated media, not documentation of an actual tree's developmental history.

## What should have happened

The assistant should have pursued two lines of inquiry in parallel:

- **Causal plausibility:** reconstruct the sequence required to produce the depicted final state, checking intermediate mechanical, biological, and environmental constraints.
- **Provenance:** inspect the original post and source context to determine whether the image/video documented a real event, a deliberate installation, a staged scene, or synthetic media.

A plausible mechanism for one feature is not an explanation of the whole scene. A final state that looks possible is not evidence that the implied trajectory is possible. Likewise, disproving a natural explanation would not alone prove AI fabrication; provenance needed independent verification.

## Critical-thinking lesson

**Reconstruct the causal trajectory, not just the final state.** When the explanation depends on a sequence of changes, test whether the intermediate states are viable under the constraints at each stage. Track changing geometry, loads, resources, capabilities, and environmental conditions. If the timeline does not reconcile, preserve the mismatch and investigate it rather than patching the story with another possibility.

Also investigate provenance when authenticity is uncertain, in parallel with causal analysis. Keep evidence about what a source is separate from evidence about whether the depicted events are physically plausible.

## Process assessment

The final identification of the AI label was reached only after the user challenged the assistant's assumptions and located the source post. The assistant did not independently notice the provenance clue early, and it initially failed to apply the causal-timeline reasoning that ultimately clarified the mismatch. This case records a failure and a useful correction; it is not evidence that the skill has become reliably autonomous.
