## Prompt

Why does a zipper work?

## Version A — Ordinary first-draft answer

A zipper works through the interaction of two rows of interlocking elements mounted on fabric tapes and a slider containing a Y-shaped channel. As the slider moves in the closing direction, its internal geometry guides the two rows towards one another, causing their elements to engage sequentially and form a continuous chain. Moving the slider in the opposite direction separates the rows, with the central wedge forcing the elements apart. The effectiveness of this mechanism depends on precise element geometry and slider dimensions, since even small deviations can prevent proper engagement or allow the closed chain to separate under tension.

## Version B — Automaticity rewrite

[Automaticity used]

A zipper has two rows of teeth, one on each fabric strip. The slider joins or separates these rows as you pull it.

Inside the slider is a passage shaped like a Y.

- **To close:** The two rows enter separate arms of the passage and are guided together. Their teeth fit into one another, forming a joined chain.
- **To open:** A wedge inside the slider pushes the rows apart, releasing the teeth one by one.

The teeth and slider must be made to very precise dimensions. If the teeth don't fit properly, the zipper may split open even when it appears closed.

The clever part is that the slider does not lock the entire zipper at once. It joins or separates the teeth progressively as it moves.

## Finding

**Provisional positive result for this writing probe.** The rewrite makes the slider's two operations easier to follow by separating them into a closing sequence and an opening sequence. It keeps the central mechanical details and the manufacturing-tolerance qualification.

The improvement is not simply shorter wording. It is a clearer order of operations and more familiar names for the parts. The added final sentence restates the progressive action; it may be redundant and is a candidate for trimming.
